# **Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

## **1\. Product Overview**

### **Working Product Description**

A personal productivity and automation assistant that helps people turn goals and intentions into small, actionable daily steps, while reducing the mental burden of planning, remembering, organizing, communicating, and following through.

The product is designed for anyone who struggles with organization and follow-through, with particular consideration for people with ADHD and executive-function challenges.

The core philosophy is:

> **Big goals become achievable when they are broken into small actions that are visible, manageable, and completed consistently.**

The product combines:

* Goal planning  
* Goal breakdown  
* Daily task management  
* Progress visualization  
* Reminders  
* Workflow assistance  
* Email assistance  
* Calendar and scheduling assistance  
* Repetitive-task automation  
* Personalized AI assistance

It is not intended to simply be another task-management application.

Its primary purpose is to help users move from:

**“I need to do this.”**

to:

**“I know exactly what I need to do next, and I can actually do it.”**

# **2\. Problem Statement**

Many people have goals, responsibilities, appointments, emails, projects, and routines they want to stay on top of.

The problem is often not knowing *what* they want.

The problem is the execution gap between:

**Intention → Planning → Action → Follow-through → Completion**

Users may struggle with:

* Breaking large goals into manageable actions  
* Knowing where to start  
* Remembering tasks once they are out of sight  
* Determining what should be done today  
* Feeling overwhelmed by large or vague tasks  
* Keeping track of multiple commitments  
* Repeatedly performing the same administrative tasks  
* Responding to emails consistently  
* Scheduling appointments  
* Remembering to send follow-ups  
* Maintaining momentum toward long-term goals  
* Seeing how today's actions contribute to larger objectives

Traditional productivity tools often require the user to do much of the organizational thinking themselves.

This creates an additional burden for the very people who need organizational support most.

# **3\. Product Vision**

To create a personal execution assistant that helps people consistently turn their intentions into meaningful progress.

The product should feel like a combination of:

**Planner \+ Personal Assistant \+ Accountability Partner \+ Automation Assistant**

without requiring the user to become an expert at productivity systems.

# **4\. Target Users**

## **Primary User**

Adults who struggle with:

* Organization  
* Planning  
* Task initiation  
* Follow-through  
* Remembering commitments  
* Prioritization  
* Managing multiple responsibilities  
* Turning large goals into actionable steps

The product should be particularly useful for people with ADHD or executive-function challenges, but should not require an ADHD diagnosis or identity.

## **Example Users**

### **The Professional**

Has several work responsibilities and personal goals but frequently loses track of follow-ups, emails and small tasks.

### **The Entrepreneur**

Has ambitious business goals but becomes overwhelmed trying to determine what needs to happen each day.

### **The Student**

Has academic goals and deadlines but struggles to translate them into consistent daily study actions.

### **The Busy Adult**

Has personal goals, appointments, errands and responsibilities competing for attention.

### **The ADHD User**

Knows what they want to accomplish but frequently experiences:

* “Out of sight, out of mind”  
* Difficulty starting  
* Overwhelm  
* Forgetting intermediate steps  
* Losing momentum  
* Difficulty estimating what can realistically be done in a day

# **5\. Core Product Principle**

## **1% Progress**

The product should reinforce the idea that meaningful progress does not require completing everything at once.

A large goal should continuously be translated into smaller achievable actions.

For example:

**Goal:** Write a book.

↓

**Yearly outcome:** Complete and publish the book.

↓

**Monthly outcome:** Complete the first four chapters.

↓

**Weekly objective:** Complete Chapter 2\.

↓

**Today's action:** Draft the introduction.

↓

**Tiny action:** Write the first paragraph.

The user should be able to see that completing the small action is not an isolated task.

It contributes to the larger goal.

### **Product philosophy**

> **Every small action should have context.**

The user should understand:

**Why am I doing this?**

**What does it contribute to?**

**What comes next?**

# **6\. Core User Experience**

The primary user journey should be:

### **1\. Tell the product what you want**

The user enters a goal naturally.

Example:

> “I want to launch my online coaching business this year.”

### **2\. Product creates a suggested plan**

The product proposes:

* Major milestones  
* Monthly outcomes  
* Weekly objectives  
* Daily actions

### **3\. User reviews the plan**

The user can:

* Accept it  
* Edit it  
* Remove items  
* Add information  
* Change deadlines  
* Adjust priorities

### **4\. Product creates today's focus**

Instead of presenting an overwhelming list of everything the user needs to do, the product identifies the most relevant actions for today.

### **5\. User completes small actions**

Each completed action contributes to the relevant goal.

### **6\. Product keeps the goal visible**

The user can see progress from:

**Today's actions → Weekly progress → Monthly progress → Overall goal**

### **7\. Product helps execute repetitive work**

Where appropriate, the assistant can help with:

* Emails  
* Scheduling  
* Reminders  
* Follow-ups  
* Repetitive workflows

# **7\. Core Features**

## **Feature 1: Goal Creation**

Users can create goals using natural language.

Examples:

* “I want to get fit.”  
* “I want to launch my business.”  
* “I want to save ₦2 million.”  
* “I want to complete my professional certification.”  
* “I want to organize my home.”

The user should not have to know how to structure the goal.

The product should help clarify it when necessary.

### **Goal information may include:**

* Desired outcome  
* Target date  
* Importance  
* Relevant context  
* Existing commitments  
* User-defined milestones

# **8\. Feature 2: Intelligent Goal Breakdown**

This is one of the product's core differentiators.

The assistant automatically creates a suggested path from the user's goal to daily action.

### **Structure**

**Long-term goal**

↓

**Milestones**

↓

**Monthly outcomes**

↓

**Weekly objectives**

↓

**Daily actions**

↓

**Next tiny step**

The product should avoid creating unnecessarily complicated plans.

The goal is not to create more tasks.

The goal is to create **clarity**.

### **Example**

User:

> “I want to start exercising regularly.”

Instead of:

> Exercise more.

The product might create:

**Goal:** Establish a consistent exercise routine.

**Monthly outcome:** Exercise consistently for four weeks.

**Weekly objective:** Complete three workouts.

**Today:** Complete a 20-minute workout.

**Next action:** Put on workout clothes.

The product should be capable of making actions smaller when the user is struggling to start.

# **9\. Feature 3: Plan Review and Collaboration**

The product should not assume its first plan is perfect.

After generating a plan, the assistant should allow the user to say things such as:

* “This is too much.”  
* “I only have two hours on Saturdays.”  
* “Move this to next month.”  
* “I don't need to do this.”  
* “Break this task down further.”  
* “What should I do first?”  
* “I have fallen behind. Help me restart.”

The assistant should adapt the plan rather than treating the original plan as fixed.

# **10\. Feature 4: Daily Focus**

The product should have a strong **Today** experience.

Instead of showing the user every responsibility they have ever created, it should answer:

> **“What do I actually need to focus on today?”**

The daily view should show:

### **Today's priorities**

A small number of meaningful actions.

### **Quick wins**

Tasks that can be completed easily and help create momentum.

### **Scheduled commitments**

Appointments, meetings and time-sensitive activities.

### **Goal connection**

The user can see which larger goal each important action contributes toward.

### **Progress**

A simple visual indication of progress made during the day.

The product should avoid overwhelming users with an enormous task list.

**11\. Feature 5: Task Simplification**

Any task should be capable of being broken into smaller actions.

For example:

**Task: Prepare presentation**

could become:

1. Review presentation requirements  
2. Identify key topics  
3. Gather supporting information  
4. Create outline  
5. Draft slides  
6. Review slides  
7. Practice presentation

If the user still feels overwhelmed:

> “Break this down further.”

The assistant should continue until it reaches a sufficiently small, actionable step.

The objective is:

> **Make the next action obvious.**

# **12\. Feature 6: Progress Visualization**

The product should make progress visible.

A user should be able to see:

**Today's progress**

↓

**This week's progress**

↓

**This month's progress**

↓

**Overall goal progress**

The visual experience should reinforce:

> “What I do today matters.”

Progress should not be based solely on the number of tasks completed.

The product should also communicate meaningful movement toward the user's intended outcome.

# **13\. Feature 7: Smart Reminders**

Reminders should be designed around **follow-through**, not simply notifications.

Instead of only saying:

> “Task due today.”

The assistant should provide useful context.

For example:

> “You planned to contact three potential clients this week. You have contacted one. Would you like to handle the next one now?”

Reminders should be:

* Contextual  
* Action-oriented  
* Timely  
* Helpful rather than nagging  
* Adjustable by the user

The product should also recognize that repeated reminders that are consistently ignored may indicate that the task needs to be reconsidered, rescheduled, or broken down.

**14\. Feature 8: Goal Recovery**

Users will inevitably fall behind.

The product should not make users feel that they have “failed.”

Instead, the user should be able to say:

> “I've fallen behind.”

The assistant should help them:

1. Review what was missed  
2. Identify what still matters  
3. Remove or reschedule unnecessary tasks  
4. Rebuild the upcoming plan  
5. Identify the next manageable action

The product should support **restarting without rebuilding everything from scratch**.

# **15\. Feature 9: Personalized Email Assistant**

The email assistant should go beyond generic AI-generated responses.

The product should learn the user's preferred communication style from information the user chooses to provide or use.

It should understand characteristics such as:

* Tone  
* Formality  
* Typical greetings  
* Sign-offs  
* Sentence style  
* Preferred wording  
* Common templates  
* Communication preferences

### **Example**

Instead of:

> “Here is a professionally worded response.”

The product should aim for:

> “This sounds like something you would actually send.”

Users should be able to define or correct their preferred style.

The assistant should also support common workflows such as:

* Drafting responses  
* Following up  
* Acknowledging messages  
* Requesting information  
* Confirming appointments  
* Sending reminders  
* Using saved templates

The user should remain in control of sending important communications.

# **16\. Feature 10: Scheduling Assistant**

The assistant should help users manage scheduling-related tasks.

Examples:

> “Schedule a meeting with Sarah next week.”

> “Find a time for my weekly team meeting.”

> “Remind me to follow up with John in three days.”

> “Set up an appointment and send the invitation.”

The product should reduce the number of individual steps the user has to remember.

# **17\. Feature 11: Workflow Automation**

Users should be able to describe repetitive processes in plain language.

For example:

> “Every Monday I need to review my weekly goals, check outstanding emails, send client follow-ups and plan my week.”

The product can turn this into a repeatable workflow.

### **Example workflow**

**Monday Weekly Reset**

1. Review previous week's goals  
2. Identify unfinished tasks  
3. Review upcoming appointments  
4. Review important emails  
5. Identify weekly priorities  
6. Schedule important activities  
7. Select daily actions

The user should be able to reuse the workflow rather than reconstructing it every week.

# **18\. Feature 12: Personal Templates**

Users should be able to create reusable templates for recurring activities.

Examples:

### **Email templates**

* Meeting confirmation  
* Follow-up  
* Client check-in  
* Request for information  
* Thank-you message

### **Personal workflows**

* Weekly planning  
* Monthly review  
* Project launch  
* Travel preparation  
* Job application process

### **Goal templates**

* Learning a skill  
* Starting a business  
* Fitness goal  
* Financial goal  
* Certification preparation

Templates should reduce repeated thinking.

# **19\. Feature 13: Personal Assistant Interaction**

The assistant should allow users to interact naturally rather than forcing them to navigate multiple screens.

Users should be able to say:

> “What should I focus on today?”

> “I'm overwhelmed. Help me decide what to do first.”

> “Break this task down.”

> “Move this to next week.”

> “What am I forgetting?”

> “Why is this on my list?”

> “I don't have enough time today. Rework my plan.”

> “I finished this. What's next?”

The assistant becomes the user's interface for managing their commitments.

# **20\. Feature 14: At-a-Glance Dashboard**

The dashboard should answer four questions immediately:

### **1\. What am I working toward?**

The user's active goals.

### **2\. What matters today?**

Today's priority actions.

### **3\. What is coming up?**

Important deadlines, appointments and commitments.

### **4\. Am I making progress?**

Visual progress toward active goals.

The dashboard should prioritize **clarity over information density**.

# **21\. Feature 15: Focus Protection**

Because distraction and context switching can undermine execution, the product should help users maintain focus.

When a user chooses a task, the experience should make the next action clear.

For example:

> **Goal:** Launch business  
> **Today:** Contact 2 potential customers  
> **Next:** Send this message to Sarah

The product should avoid continuously presenting unrelated tasks when the user is trying to complete the current one.

# **22\. Feature 16: Flexible Planning**

The product should recognize that real life changes.

Users should be able to say:

> “I can't do this today.”

Instead of treating this as failure, the assistant should help determine:

* Should it move?  
* Should it be broken down?  
* Should something else be deprioritized?  
* Does the goal need to change?  
* Can another task be completed instead?

The system should help users **re-plan without losing the bigger picture**.

# **23\. Feature 17: Accountability Without Pressure**

The product should encourage progress without becoming punitive.

The tone should be:

> “Let's figure out what happened and what we can do next.”

Not:

> “You missed three tasks.”

The assistant should celebrate completed progress while helping users recover from missed commitments.

# **24\. Information Architecture**

The product should revolve around a small number of core areas:

### **Today**

What needs attention now.

### **Goals**

What the user is ultimately trying to achieve.

### **Tasks**

The actionable steps required to move forward.

### **Calendar**

Appointments, deadlines and scheduled commitments.

### **Automations**

Recurring workflows and repetitive actions.

### **Assistant**

Natural-language interaction with the user's personal productivity assistant.

The user should not need to understand complex productivity terminology to use the product.

# **25\. Primary User Journey**

### **Step 1 — User creates a goal**

> “I want to become a certified data analyst.”

### **Step 2 — Assistant asks useful clarifying questions**

For example:

* When would you like to achieve this?  
* How much time can you realistically dedicate?  
* Do you already have relevant knowledge?

### **Step 3 — Assistant creates a proposed roadmap**

**Goal**

→ Major milestones  
→ Monthly outcomes  
→ Weekly objectives  
→ Daily actions

### **Step 4 — User reviews and adjusts it**

The user approves or modifies the plan.

### **Step 5 — Product creates the user's daily focus**

The user sees a manageable set of actions.

### **Step 6 — User completes actions**

Each action contributes toward the larger goal.

### **Step 7 — Product keeps the user aware**

Reminders, upcoming commitments and progress are surfaced at appropriate times.

### **Step 8 — Product adapts**

When circumstances change, the user can ask the assistant to re-plan.

# **26\. Example End-to-End Experience**

### **User goal**

> “I want to launch my coaching business by December.”

### **Assistant**

**Let's break this down.**

Suggested milestones:

1. Define coaching offer  
2. Identify target audience  
3. Create service structure  
4. Develop brand presence  
5. Set up client acquisition process  
6. Get first clients  
7. Launch

### **Monthly**

**September:** Define offer and target audience

### **Weekly**

**Week 1:** Define ideal client

### **Today**

**Action:** Write down three types of people you most want to help.

### **User**

> “That feels like too much.”

### **Assistant**

> “Let's make it smaller. For the next five minutes, write down just one person you would most enjoy helping.”

### **User completes it.**

The product records progress toward the weekly and monthly objective.

This is the core emotional experience the product should create:

> **“I don't have to figure everything out right now. I only need to know what to do next.”**

# **28\. What the Product Should NOT Become**

To maintain focus, the product should avoid becoming:

* A complicated project-management platform  
* A generic AI chatbot  
* A basic to-do list  
* A calendar replacement  
* An overwhelming productivity dashboard  
* A rigid productivity methodology  
* A system that requires users to constantly maintain their own system

The product should **do more organizational thinking for the user**, not create more organizational work.

# **29\. Key Differentiator**

The strongest differentiator is not simply AI.

It is the combination of:

### **Understanding the goal**

**What do you want to accomplish?**

* **Understanding the person**

**How do you naturally work and communicate?**

* **Understanding the next action**

**What is the smallest useful thing you can do now?**

* **Helping execute**

**Can the product remind, schedule, draft, organize or automate this for you?**

* **Keeping the goal visible**

**How does today's action contribute to the bigger outcome?**

This creates a continuous loop:

> **Goal → Breakdown → Action → Reminder → Completion → Progress → Adaptation**

# **30\. Product Success Metrics**

The product should ultimately be measured by whether users **make meaningful progress**, rather than simply how many tasks they create.

Important indicators include:

* Goals created  
* Goals actively worked on  
* Daily actions completed  
* Percentage of planned actions completed  
* Goals progressing over time  
* Users returning to their goals  
* Tasks successfully completed after reminders  
* Repetitive workflows successfully handled  
* Emails successfully drafted or assisted  
* Users recovering after falling behind  
* User-reported reduction in overwhelm  
* User-reported improvement in follow-through

A particularly important measure should be:

> **How often does the product help a user move from intention to completed action?**

# **31\. Product Personality**

The product should feel:

* Calm  
* Helpful  
* Intelligent  
* Encouraging  
* Non-judgmental  
* Practical

* Overly enthusiastic  
* Guilt-inducing

The product's central promise can be expressed as:

# **Appendix — Requested Changes (added 2026-09-27, pending implementation)**

Recorded verbatim from product owner. Spec body above is unchanged; these items are to be designed and built.

## **1. Buttons — remove the defer option**

- Remove the "defer" choice (e.g. the "Not today" / quiet button in `design.html`).
- Buttons should only move the user forward (complete, break down, start) — no opt-out that quietly drops the action.
- Open question for design: where does a genuinely undoable day go (replan via Assistant only, no one-tap defer)?

## **2. Missing visibility — daily progress status bar + at-a-glance goals dashboard**

- Add a **daily progress status bar** (today's completion visible wherever the user works).
- Add/strengthen an **at-a-glance dashboard** showing the goals the user is actively working on.
- Reason: "out of sight, out of mind" — active goals must stay visible so momentum survives busy days.
- Implication for `design.html` and Phase 2/7: status bar component + dashboard goals-at-a-glance section are required, not optional.

## **3. Task automation must be explicit and visible**

Owner asked where the automation of tasks is, specifically:
- **Calendar scheduling automation** (propose slots, create events + invites).
- **Reminder automation** (contextual follow-through nudges, follow-up reminders).
- **Email response automation** driven by the user's **personalized template, input, and voice** (drafts that sound like the user; user stays in control of send).
- Implication: Phases 4–6 scope must stay prominent in plan and UI (Automations area + Assistant), not buried. Surface each automation with its source (template/voice/input) so the user trusts it.
